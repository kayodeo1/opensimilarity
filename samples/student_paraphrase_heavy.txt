Using Rain to Cut Hostel Water Bills

Administrators often assume that any new source of water must be fit to drink before it is worth paying for. I argue that this assumption is wrong, and that cheaper options are being overlooked by the people who plan campus budgets each year.

The metal roofs on the older halls are severely rusted, and this increases the amount of iron in the water that gets captured, so it cannot safely be consumed unless it is purified first. Even so, devices that divert the first flow of rain got rid of the majority of the grit from every storm, and the collected rainwater turned out to be good enough for toilet flushing, laundry and mopping, tasks that jointly make up over half of what a hostel uses each day.

Spending a little on gutters is therefore sensible even where a roof looks too old to be useful. Bursars should compare this modest outlay with the recurring diesel bill before dismissing the idea, because the savings arrive as soon as the rains begin.
