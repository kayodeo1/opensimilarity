Borehole Maintenance on a Nigerian Campus

Boreholes remain the backbone of campus water in much of the country, yet few institutions keep proper records of how they perform. This essay reviews what a simple logbook could reveal about pump failures.

Technicians at the works department told me that most breakdowns begin with worn seals, which allow sand into the pump chamber. Replacing a seal takes one afternoon, while replacing a burnt-out motor can take a month because the part must be ordered from Lagos. A logbook recording the hours each pump has run would let the department replace seals before they fail.

Water quality testing is a separate concern. Samples taken twice a year would be enough to notice contamination from nearby septic tanks, provided somebody is made responsible for acting on the results. Without a named officer the reports tend to sit unread in a filing cabinet.
