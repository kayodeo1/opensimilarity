Teaching Fractions with Market Prices

Children who struggle with fractions on the chalkboard frequently handle them without difficulty at a market stall. This reflection describes a short classroom experiment that drew on that everyday competence.

Each group received a pretend budget and a list of prices for tomatoes, peppers and rice sold in halves and quarters of a measure. Pupils argued over the cheapest combination and checked one another's sums on scraps of paper. By the end of the week several who had previously refused to attempt written exercises were volunteering answers.

The approach has limits. Some topics, such as dividing one fraction by another, do not map neatly onto buying and selling, and a teacher needs other illustrations for those lessons. Still, starting from what pupils already do outside school made the formal notation feel far less forbidding.
